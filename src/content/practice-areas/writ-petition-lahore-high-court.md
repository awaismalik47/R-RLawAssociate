## What a writ petition in Lahore High Court can do

Article 199 of the Constitution of Pakistan gives the High Court power to make orders against public authorities where "no other adequate remedy is provided by law". The traditional names for these orders, called writs, describe what the court can do:

- **Mandamus**: directing an official or department to do something the law requires, such as deciding a pending application, issuing a document or releasing dues;
- **Prohibition**: stopping an authority from doing something it has no legal power to do;
- **Certiorari**: declaring that an order already passed is without lawful authority and of no legal effect;
- **Habeas corpus**: requiring a person held in custody to be produced before the court so that the legality of the detention can be examined;
- **Quo warranto**: requiring a person holding public office to show under what authority they hold it.

The court can also enforce **fundamental rights** guaranteed by the Constitution, such as the right to fair trial and due process, equality before the law, and protection of property.

## When you may need a writ petition

People usually come to us after an official process has failed them. Common situations include:

- a government department, authority or university has ignored an application for months, or refused it without reasons;
- an order has been passed against you without a hearing (for example a cancellation, demolition notice, blacklisting or withdrawal of a licence);
- police or another agency is **harassing** you without lawful basis, or a relative is being **detained illegally**;
- an examination result, admission decision or recruitment process was conducted in breach of its own rules;
- a public body is enforcing a tax, fee or condition that the law does not allow;
- a lower court or tribunal has acted entirely outside its jurisdiction and no appeal is available.

Not every grievance is suitable for a writ. If the law gives you an appeal, a revision or a tribunal, the High Court will normally expect you to use it first. Government employees who are civil servants usually go to the Service Tribunal rather than the High Court; see our page on [service matters in Lahore](/practice-areas/service-matters-lawyer-lahore/). Complaints that the police will not register an FIR are normally taken first to the Justice of Peace (the Sessions Judge) under Sections 22-A and 22-B of the Code of Criminal Procedure; our [criminal lawyers in Lahore](/practice-areas/criminal-lawyer-lahore/) can advise on that route.

## How we handle writ petitions

1. **Test maintainability first.** We check whether the respondent is a public authority, whether an alternative remedy exists, and whether there has been delay. A frank answer at this stage saves time and court fees.
2. **Build the record.** A writ is decided mainly on documents. We collect the orders, applications, notices and correspondence, and where necessary send a written representation to the department so the record shows you asked for relief before coming to court.
3. **Draft precisely.** The petition sets out the facts, the legal grounds and the exact orders sought, with an application for interim relief where urgent.
4. **Appear at admission.** At the first hearing (the "motion" or admission stage) the court decides whether to issue notice to the respondents. We prepare a short, clear argument for this hearing.
5. **Follow through to compliance.** After a judgment we help ensure the department implements it, and if it does not, we can file a **contempt** application.

## Documents usually required

- your CNIC and, for a company, its incorporation documents and a board resolution;
- the order, notice or letter you are challenging, or proof of the application that has not been decided;
- any representation, appeal or reminder you have already sent, with proof of delivery;
- the relevant rules, policy or advertisement (for example for admissions or recruitment);
- for habeas corpus: details of who detained the person, when and where, and any FIR or detention order;
- a short timeline of events in your own words.

## Typical process and timeline

| Stage | What happens | Usual time |
|---|---|---|
| Review | Maintainability check and collection of documents | A few days |
| Filing | Petition filed at the Lahore High Court, Lahore (or the relevant bench) | Once documents are ready |
| Admission hearing | Court decides whether to issue notice; interim relief may be granted | Often within days of filing, sooner if urgent |
| Reply | Government respondents file report and parawise comments | Several weeks to months |
| Final hearing | Arguments on the merits | Depends on the court's list |
| Implementation | Compliance by the department; contempt application if needed | After judgment |

Habeas corpus and other urgent petitions can be fixed for hearing very quickly. Petitions about delayed decisions are sometimes disposed of at the first hearing with a direction to the department to decide the matter within a fixed time.

## Writs in property and land matters

Orders of development authorities, cantonment boards, revenue officers and the Board of Revenue are often challenged through constitutional petitions once the statutory appeals are exhausted. If your dispute is really about ownership between private parties, however, a civil suit is usually the proper remedy.
