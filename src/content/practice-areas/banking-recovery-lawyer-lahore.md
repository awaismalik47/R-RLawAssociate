## When you may need a banking recovery lawyer in Lahore

Banking litigation is technical and fast-moving. You may want to speak to a banking recovery lawyer in Lahore if:

- you have received a **legal notice** or **summons** from a Banking Court for a loan, running finance, car lease, credit card or mortgage facility;
- the bank has issued a **notice of sale** of mortgaged property, or a property has been auctioned and you believe the process was irregular;
- you are a **guarantor** and are being sued for a loan you did not take;
- the bank's statement of account includes **charges or mark-up** that you dispute;
- your business has lent money or supplied goods on credit and needs to **recover dues** through the courts;
- you want to negotiate a **settlement or rescheduling** before the matter escalates.

## How banking recovery cases work

Most cases between banks and their customers fall under the **Financial Institutions (Recovery of Finances) Ordinance 2001**, often called FIO 2001. It sets up special Banking Courts and a summary procedure designed to be quicker than an ordinary civil suit.

The most important feature for a defendant is **leave to defend**. Instead of an ordinary written statement, the defendant must apply for leave to defend within **30 days** of being served. The application must state:

- the amount of finance the customer actually received;
- the amounts repaid, with dates;
- the amount the customer says is really due, if any;
- the specific facts and legal grounds on which the claim is disputed, with supporting documents.

If the application does not meet these requirements, the court can reject it and decree the suit. Missing the deadline, or filing a vague application, is one of the most common reasons people lose banking cases.

## Documents usually required

- the summons, plaint and annexures served on you, with the envelope or proof of the date of service;
- sanction or offer letters, finance agreements, and any letters of guarantee or hypothecation;
- mortgage documents and title documents of the property offered as security;
- your own record of payments: deposit slips, bank statements and receipts;
- correspondence with the bank, including any rescheduling or settlement proposals;
- notices of sale or auction, and any advertisement published in the newspaper.

| Stage | What happens | Usual time |

| Leave to defend | Application filed with figures and documents | Within 30 days of service |

## Mortgaged property and auctions

When finance is secured on property, the risk of losing that property is often the client's main concern. The law gives banks strong powers, but it also lays down notices and procedures that must be followed. If you have received a notice, do not wait for the auction date.
