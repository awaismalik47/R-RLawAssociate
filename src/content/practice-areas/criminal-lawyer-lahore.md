## When you may need a criminal lawyer in Lahore

You may need a criminal lawyer in Lahore if:

- a family member has been **arrested** and you need to apply for bail;
- you have learned that an **FIR** (First Information Report) has been registered against you and you fear arrest;
- the police are **refusing to register** your complaint;
- you have been called to join an investigation and want advice on what to say and which documents to produce;
- a **challan** (the police report under Section 173 of the Code of Criminal Procedure) has been submitted and the trial is about to begin;
- you have been convicted and want to consider an **appeal** or **revision**;
- you are the complainant and want to follow a case properly, including objecting to bail.

## Bail and pre-arrest bail (zamanat)

Bail is the most common urgent need in criminal practice. The law distinguishes between:

- **Bailable offences** (Section 496 CrPC), where bail is a right and is usually granted by the police or the Magistrate on furnishing surety.
- **Non-bailable offences** (Section 497 CrPC), where bail is at the court's discretion. The court considers whether there are "reasonable grounds" to believe the accused committed the offence, whether the case calls for "further inquiry", the punishment provided, and the conduct of the accused. Separate considerations apply to women, juveniles, the sick and those who have been in custody for long periods.
- **Pre-arrest bail** (Section 498 CrPC), which protects a person from arrest. Courts treat this as an extraordinary relief and generally expect the applicant to show that the case appears to be motivated by mala fide (bad faith) or ulterior motive, for example to humiliate or harass.

Bail is usually sought first in the court of the Magistrate or Sessions Judge, and then in the Lahore High Court if refused. A bail lawyer in Lahore will normally follow this order because the High Court expects lower courts to be approached first unless there are exceptional reasons. ## How we handle criminal matters

1. **Quick assessment.** We read the FIR and any documents, identify the offences and whether they are bailable, and explain the realistic options.
2. **Immediate steps.** Where arrest is feared, we prepare a pre-arrest bail petition with an affidavit and supporting documents, and accompany the client to court. Where a person is in custody, we apply for post-arrest bail and attend the hearing.
3. **Investigation stage.** We advise on joining the investigation, producing evidence of innocence, and applications to transfer the investigation where appropriate.
4. **Trial.** We prepare the defence, cross-examine prosecution witnesses, and file applications for acquittal under Section 249-A or 265-K CrPC where the evidence does not support a conviction.
5. **Appeals and revisions.** We prepare appeals against conviction or acquittal before the Sessions Court or the Lahore High Court.

## Documents usually required

- a copy of the **FIR**, if available (you can often obtain it from the police station or online through the Punjab Police services);
- CNIC copies of the accused and of the proposed surety;
- any earlier bail orders, court orders or notices;
- documents that support your version of events (agreements, receipts, messages, medical reports, travel records);
- the **challan** and witness statements once the case reaches trial;
- for sureties, documents showing ownership of property or a government salary, as required by the court.

## Typical process and timeline

| Stage | What happens | Usual time |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-arrest bail | Petition filed; court may grant interim bail and call for the police record | First hearing within days; confirmation in a few weeks |
| Post-arrest bail | Application in the Magistrate's or Sessions Court; then High Court if refused | Often decided within one to three weeks per forum |
| Investigation | Police collect evidence and submit the challan | Varies, typically weeks to months |
| Trial | Charge framed, prosecution evidence, defence evidence, arguments | Several months to a few years |
| Appeal | Filed within the limitation period after judgment | Depends on the court's list |

These times are general indications only. We will explain the likely course of your case after reviewing the record.
