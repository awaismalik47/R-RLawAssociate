## When you may need a civil lawyer in Lahore

Civil disputes are about rights and obligations between people, companies and sometimes government bodies. You may need a civil lawyer in Lahore if:

- someone owes you money under a loan, a sale, a cheque or a business arrangement and will not pay;
- a party to a contract or **agreement to sell** is refusing to complete the transaction and you want **specific performance**;
- you need a **declaration** from the court about your legal status or title, for example that a document is void or that you are the owner of property;
- you need an urgent **injunction** (stay order) to stop someone from selling, constructing on or interfering with property;
- you have been sued and need to file a written statement and defend the case;
- you want to **execute** a decree that you have already obtained;
- you want to challenge a judgment through an **appeal** or **revision**.

## Common civil remedies explained

- **Suit for recovery:** a claim for a sum of money owed, with or without profit or damages. Suits on cheques and promissory notes can proceed under the summary procedure in Order XXXVII CPC.
- **Declaration (Section 42, Specific Relief Act 1877):** the court declares a legal character or right to property. It is often combined with other relief.
- **Permanent injunction (Section 54):** a final order restraining a party from doing an act.
- **Temporary injunction (Order XXXIX CPC):** an interim order during the case, granted only if the three tests of prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable loss are met.
- **Specific performance (Section 12):** an order to complete a contract, typically an agreement to sell immovable property, where money compensation would not be adequate.
- **Cancellation of documents (Section 39):** where a document such as a sale deed or gift deed is void or voidable and may cause serious injury if left standing.

## How we handle civil cases

1. **Review the documents.** Civil cases are won or lost on documents and limitation. We start by reading every agreement, receipt, notice and record you have.
2. **Advise on options.** We explain whether a legal notice, negotiation, mediation, arbitration or a suit is the sensible first step, and the likely costs and timescales of each.
3. **Draft and file.** We draft the plaint or written statement, prepare the list of documents and witnesses, and apply for interim relief where necessary.
4. **Conduct the trial.** We frame issues, lead evidence, cross-examine witnesses and argue the case.
5. **Execute or appeal.** After judgment, we help enforce the decree or challenge it in appeal or revision.

## Documents usually required

- the agreement, contract, receipt, cheque or promissory note on which the claim is based;
- any **legal notices** sent or received, with courier or postal receipts;
- title documents for property cases (registry, fard, mutation, allotment or transfer letters);
- correspondence such as letters, emails and WhatsApp messages;
- copies of earlier court orders, if any;
- CNIC copies and, for companies, a board resolution authorising the case.

## Typical process and timeline

| Stage | What happens | Usual time |
|---|---|---|
| Legal notice | Formal demand and opportunity to settle | 2–4 weeks |
| Filing | Plaint, court fee and documents filed; interim application heard | First hearing within days to weeks |
| Pleadings | Defendant files written statement; issues framed | 2–6 months |
| Evidence | Each side produces witnesses and documents | Several months or longer |
| Judgment | Arguments heard and decision announced | Depends on the court's list |
| Execution / appeal | Enforcement of decree or appeal to higher court | Varies |
