## When you may need a khula lawyer in Lahore

Family disputes are personal and often urgent. You may want to speak to a family or khula lawyer in Lahore if:

- you wish to end your marriage through **khula** (dissolution of marriage by the court at the wife's request) or under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act 1939 on grounds such as cruelty or non-payment of maintenance;
- your husband has pronounced **talaq** and you need to know whether the Union Council notice has been sent and when the divorce becomes effective;
- you need to **pronounce talaq** yourself and want it done correctly under Section 7 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961, including notice to the Chairman of the Union Council;
- you want to claim or defend a case for **child custody** (hizanat), visitation (meeting schedule) or guardianship of a minor;
- you need **maintenance** (nan-o-nafqa) for yourself or your children, or the amount fixed earlier needs to be revised;
- you want to recover **dowry articles** (jahez), gifts or **haq mehr** (dower) after separation;
- you have received notice of a family suit and need to file a written statement in time.

Many clients first want to understand their options before deciding anything. A consultation is the right place to talk through reconciliation, mediation and court proceedings in confidence.

## How we handle family cases

Family cases in Lahore are filed in the Family Courts under the West Pakistan Family Courts Act 1964, as amended for Punjab. These courts are designed to work faster than ordinary civil courts. Our approach is calm and practical:

1. **Listen first.** We take a full account of the marriage, children, finances and any earlier proceedings, and we tell you plainly what the law allows.
2. **Choose the right claim.** Khula, dissolution on specific grounds, custody, maintenance and dowry are often combined in one suit, which saves time and court fees. We advise on what to join together and what to keep separate.
3. **Draft carefully.** The plaint (the written claim) sets the direction of the whole case, so we draft it with the documents attached and a clear list of reliefs.
4. **Attend the reconciliation stage.** The Family Court must try to reconcile the parties before trial. We prepare you for this hearing and explain what will happen.
5. **Follow through.** After a decree, we assist with the Union Council process for the effectiveness certificate, execution of maintenance or dowry decrees, and appeals where needed.

Where a family dispute touches on property, for example a house given as haq mehr or a share in inherited land, we work with our [property and inheritance lawyers in Lahore](/practice-areas/property-lawyer-lahore/). If the other side has filed a criminal complaint alongside the family case, our [criminal and bail lawyers](/practice-areas/criminal-lawyer-lahore/) can assist with protection from arrest.

## Documents usually required

Bring whatever you have; we can work around gaps. Commonly needed documents are:

- copies of CNICs (yours and, if available, your spouse's);
- the **nikahnama** (marriage certificate) and NADRA marriage registration certificate, if issued;
- children's **B-forms** or birth certificates, and school records for custody or maintenance claims;
- a list of dowry articles with any receipts, photographs or the list signed at the time of marriage;
- any **talaq nama**, Union Council notices or earlier court orders;
- evidence of income or expenses relevant to maintenance (salary slips, rent agreements, school fee vouchers);
- messages, medical reports or police records if you rely on cruelty or harassment.

## Typical process and timeline

Every case is different, and we will not promise a fixed timeline. The usual stages for a khula or family suit in Lahore are:

| Stage | What happens | Usual time |
|---|---|---|
| Filing | Plaint, documents and court fee submitted in the Family Court | 1–7 days after instructions |
| Service | Summons sent to the defendant by post, courier, SMS or publication | 2–6 weeks |
| Pre-trial | Court attempts reconciliation; in khula cases a decree may be passed here if reconciliation fails | Often at the first or second effective hearing |
| Interim orders | Interim maintenance for children; meeting schedule in custody cases | Early in the case |
| Evidence and arguments | Only if contested issues remain (for example, dowry or maintenance amounts) | Several months |
| Decree and after | Certified copy, Union Council effectiveness certificate, execution | 1–3 months |

For a fuller explanation of each step, read our guide on [how to file khula in Lahore](/guides/how-to-file-khula-in-lahore/). Custody and guardianship petitions under the Guardians and Wards Act 1890 follow a similar pattern but usually take longer, because the court may interview the child and call for reports.

## Talaq, khula and the Union Council

A frequent source of confusion is the difference between khula and talaq. **Talaq** is pronounced by the husband; under Section 7 of the Muslim Family Laws Ordinance he must send written notice to the Chairman of the Union Council and a copy to the wife, and the talaq takes effect ninety days after the notice unless revoked. **Khula** is granted by the court on the wife's petition. After a khula decree, a copy is sent to the Union Council, which then issues a certificate confirming the dissolution. Remarriage, NADRA status changes and passport updates generally depend on these papers being in order, so it is worth getting them right.
